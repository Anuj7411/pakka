[1 | THE PROBLEM]

Okay, imagine you tell your AI shopping agent: get me one bag of coffee, medium
roast, under fifteen hundred rupees. And it comes back with the dark roast.
Wrong coffee. But the payment still went through. Your spending limit was fine
with it. Fraud check, fine. Your bank, fine. Because not one of them read what
you actually asked for. किसी ने आपकी बात पढ़ी ही नहीं।

[2 | WHAT PAKKA MEANS]

We call this Pakka. पक्का एक हिंदी शब्द है। मतलब, ठोस। भरोसेमंद। सही तरीके से किया हुआ।
जब आप किसी दोस्त से पूछते हो, पक्का? और वो कहता है, पक्का। तो बात कन्फर्म है। And that
is the whole idea here. Before any money moves, someone should look at that cart
and say: this is pakka. This is actually what you asked for. अभी ये कोई नहीं करता।

[3 | WHY NOTHING CATCHES IT]

Look at what payment security checks today. How much you spent. What category.
Is the card stolen. All money questions. But an AI agent doesn't mess up by
overspending. It messes up by buying the wrong thing, at the right price.
इसको कोई चेक नहीं करता। So we built the thing that does.

[4 | IT WORKING]

Here it is running. On one side, what the human asked for, locked in and signed
so it can't be quietly changed. On the other, what the agent put in the shopping
cart. Pakka compares the two before the payment happens. Here's an honest agent:
right coffee, medium roast, inside budget. Everything passes, you get allow. And
only now does checkout open.

[5 | IT CATCHING A MISTAKE]

Now the interesting one. Same request, but the agent grabs the dark roast. Pakka
doesn't just throw an error. It tells you which item, and which rule broke.
Look: you asked for medium, this one is dark. साफ़ पकड़ लिया। It's not a
confidence score you have to trust. And there are three answers: allow,
escalate, block. The worst one always wins.

[6 | THE MONEY]

When a cart does pass, Pakka also decides how much money the agent is allowed to
hold. And it's capped. There's an N P C I rule for this, O-C two-twenty-eight,
and we follow it. Never unlimited. Also, the part that suggests that number
isn't the part that approves it. So the thing that wants to spend can never be
the thing that says yes.

[7 | THE PROOF]

And every decision gets signed. Pakka takes the cart, the verdict and the
amount, signs it, then links it to the one before with a hash. Edit an old
decision and the chain visibly breaks. It's not a log you have to trust me on.
You can check it yourself. If there's ever a fight about why the agent bought
something, there's a signed answer.

[8 | CHECKOUT]

And this is real, not a mockup. It runs on actual Razorpay orders, in test mode.
If the gate blocks a cart, no order gets created. There's literally no pay
button. If someone fakes a payment signature, the server rejects it. And since
it's test mode, the page tells you what to type: any phone number, any OTP, a
test card.

[9 | THE NUMBERS]

So does it actually work? We tested the whole set. Eight hundred plus shopping
carts, five kinds of mistakes, three difficulty levels, with error bars, not one
lucky screenshot. Plain code catches ninety-two percent of the bad carts, with
zero false positives. It never blocks a good cart. एक भी सही कार्ट नहीं रोका।
Near perfect on four of the five mistakes. And we're honest about the fifth:
when the swap is the right price, right size, right category, just not the thing
you meant, code alone can't see it.

[10 | THE ONE RULE]

And that's where the AI model comes in. This is the decision I'm proudest of:
the model is only allowed to raise a flag. It can pause a payment for a human.
It can never approve one, and it can never unblock one. The smartest, least
predictable part of the system is the one part we refused to let touch your
money. यही है पक्का।
